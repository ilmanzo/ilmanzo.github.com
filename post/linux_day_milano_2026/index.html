Linux Day Milano 2026 recap

Recap of my talk 'A scatola chiusa: Test Coverage di binari nativi con eBPF' at Linux Day Milano 2026
November 2, 2026 · Andrea Manzini | Translations:
  • It

Intro#

On Saturday, October 24th, 2026, I had the pleasure of attending Linux Day Milano 2026.

For those unfamiliar with it, Linux Day is the premier nationwide event in Italy dedicated to GNU/Linux, Free Software, and open culture. Promoted annually since 2001 by the Italian Linux Society (ILS) on the fourth Saturday of October, it is held simultaneously across dozens of Italian cities thanks to local Linux User Groups (LUGs), student groups, and passionate volunteers. It remains the most important grassroots gathering for open source enthusiasts and professionals in the country.

🎙️ The Talk: A scatola chiusa — Test Coverage di binari nativi con eBPF#

At 11:45 in track T.1.1, I gave my talk: “A scatola chiusa: Test Coverage di binari nativi con eBPF” (schedule).

Measuring how much code is actually executed during integration testing is easy if you own the source and can rebuild with coverage instrumentation. But what happens when dealing with closed legacy binaries, third-party software, or entire system distributions where recompiling is out of the question? Traditional instrumentation tools like Valgrind or Intel PIN introduce heavy slowdowns (5–20×) or proprietary toolchains.

In this session, I discussed how modern Linux eBPF features (particularly uprobe_multi and in-kernel first-call deduplication) change the rules of the game. I demonstrated funkoverage, an open-source pure-Go tool that captures function-level test coverage on any native ELF binary with virtually zero overhead (< 2%). We went through live demonstrations tracing standard system binaries, illustrating how kernel tracing empowers modern black-box QA on native software.
